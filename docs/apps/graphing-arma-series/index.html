Graphing ARMA series

Explore how ACF and PACF help identify time-series orders.

Choose coefficients

Enter up to three AR and three MA coefficients. Use 0 for unused lags. The highest nonzero lag in each row determines p or q; all zeros give white noise.

AR: lagged observations
MA: lagged errors

Positive and negative numbers are allowed. Changes take effect only when you press Graph it. The intercept is fixed at zero.

Coefficient edits have not been applied. Press Graph it to update the plots.

Graph it reuses the same errors to isolate coefficient changes. Use new white noise redraws the currently graphed model with fresh errors; it does not apply pending edits.

ACF and PACF use the same 200 observations and show lags 1–15. Dashed correlation bounds are approximately ±0.139 (95% white-noise reference bounds). Individual crossings can occur by chance; they do not count model terms.

How to read the plots

Start with the time plot. Trends, changing variation, or other nonstationary behavior call for care before interpreting correlations.

Population patterns for stationary, invertible models without canceling AR and MA factors
Model ACF PACF
White noise Zero at positive lags Zero at positive lags
AR(p) Tails off Cuts off after lag p
MA(q) Cuts off after lag q Tails off
ARMA(p,q), p,q > 0 Generally tails off Generally tails off

A cutoff means zero in the population, not exactly zero in a sample. Tailing off may be smooth or oscillating. Small coefficients can be hidden by sampling variation. Canceling AR and MA factors can make a model behave like one with lower orders. These plots suggest candidates; they do not prove an order.

All simulations and graphs run in your browser. Nothing is uploaded or saved. The app follows the direct-recursion simulation and sample-plot approach used in the ECON 4325 ARMA lecture examples.
